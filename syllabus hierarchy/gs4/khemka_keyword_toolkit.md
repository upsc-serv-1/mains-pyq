# Khemka Sir's Keyword Toolkit & Answer Skeleton (GS-4)

This document serves as the third core resource in Khemka Sir's GS-4 preparation set. It outlines the essential keyword toolkit for structuring ethical arguments and the five-step answer skeleton for resolving case studies.

---

## 1. Keyword Toolkit for Answers

Use these specialized terms, legal provisions, and philosophical principles to ground your arguments in established ethical and constitutional frameworks:

*   **Recusal**: Stepping away from decision-making when personal or family interests are involved.
*   **Conflict of Interest**: Situations where private interest clashes with public duty (must be resolved in favor of the public).
*   **PoSH / Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)**: Legal and institutional framework under the Prevention of Sexual Harassment at Workplace Act.
*   **Whistleblower Protection**: Safeguards shielding employees or citizens who report wrongdoing from administrative or physical retaliation.
*   **Insider Information**: Privileged information that must never be leaked for private or asymmetric gain.
*   **Presumption Against the Owner**: In cases of hazardous child labor, the legal and ethical presumption rests against the employer/owner to prove compliance.
*   **Golden-Hour Duty**: The primary civic duty to save lives during medical emergencies (the "golden hour") overriding routine procedural goals.
*   **Ayushman Bharat / PMJAY**: Grounding welfare decisions in state-backed healthcare access for the poor and vulnerable.
*   **CSR (2% under Companies Act)**: Leveraging corporate social responsibility funds for community-centric and sustainable developmental needs.
*   **Lesser of Two Evils**: The ethical framework used under emergencies to choose the action that minimizes harm when all options carry costs.
*   **Proportionality (Employment-Intensity vs. Resource Depletion)**: Balancing the need for economic jobs with the rate of resource depletion to ensure equity.
*   **Just Transition**: Managing environmental cleanups or factory closures with social support, re-skilling, and alternative livelihood guarantees for displaced workers.
*   **Polluter-Pays & Precautionary Principle**: Forcing polluting entities to internalize environmental cleanup costs, and taking proactive preventive action even in the absence of absolute scientific certainty.
*   **IEC (Information–Education–Communication)**: Using systematic social persuasion campaigns rather than coercive force to address deep-seated prejudices (e.g., untouchability, gender bias).
*   **Rehabilitation = Job + Skilling + Annuity/Stake**: Designing comprehensive rehabilitation and resettlement (R&R) policies instead of mere token cash compensation.
*   **Reasoned 'Speaking Order' (RTI Section 8 Public-Interest)**: Issuing clear, documented justifications when denying information or taking discretionary decisions, ensuring accountability.
*   **Article 19 Free Expression**: Grounding rights of journalists, whistleblowers, and citizen activists in constitutional free speech.
*   **Untouchability as a Constitutional Offence**: Enforcing Article 17 zero-tolerance policies strictly, refusing to bend to community prejudices.
*   **Distinction & Proportionality (Use of Force)**: Applying minimal and targeted force in law enforcement/security duties, ensuring civilians are distinguished from threats.
*   **End-Use Monitoring (Arms Transfer)**: Ensuring oversight mechanisms are in place so resources/materials are not diverted to unauthorized or unethical uses.
*   **Distributive / Rawlsian Justice**: Structuring policies to maximize benefits for the least-advantaged sections of society.
*   **Environmental Sustainability over Profitability**: Prioritizing ecological integrity over short-term corporate profits as a non-negotiable bottom line.

## 2. The Five-Step Answer Skeleton

Apply this systematic five-step structure to organize your case study answers:

1.  **Stakeholders**: List everyone affected by the decision (direct and indirect, including the general public and future generations).
2.  **Ethical Conflict**: Explicitly name the core clash of values (e.g., *private interest vs. public duty*, *profit vs. human dignity*, *loyalty to organization vs. rule of law*, or *national security vs. individual liberty*).
3.  **Decision**: State your clear, unambiguous choice upfront. Do not sit on the fence or use vague administrative compromises.
4.  **Justification**: Anchor your decision in a foundational ethical principle from Part A (e.g., Deontology, Utilitarianism, Rawlsian Justice) and back it with a relevant keyword/provision from the toolkit.
5.  **Trade-off**: Acknowledge the cost you accept, recognizing that honesty and ethical maturity involve enduring hardships for the sake of integrity.
